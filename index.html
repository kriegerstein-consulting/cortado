Questions

Before you request

Where does my money go?

To the charity the banker picked from our list — in full. Cortado collects the donation and forwards 100% of it. The banker receives nothing. In this prototype no money is collected yet.

How does Cortado make money?

Only through ads on the site, for example from graduate programmes, prep courses or finance tools. Ads are placed by topic, not by tracking you, and never presented as an endorsement by a banker.

How do I know a banker is who they say they are?

Cortado does not check employers — that would tie a private activity to the banker's workplace. Instead, every banker signs a self-declaration, and after every chat the student rates it. Ratings are public and anonymous, and only students with a confirmed chat can leave one. Profiles with repeated poor ratings or reports are hidden by our team.

Is this a conflict of interest for bankers?

Bankers take part privately and receive no payment or other benefit. They make no promises about jobs or referrals and do not take part in a hiring decision about a student they met here (or disclose the contact). Chats cover careers and recruiting only — never confidential deal, client or trade information.

What if a banker cancels?

Your slot is released, no donation is collected, and you can request another open slot with the same banker or someone else.

Why don't profiles show names?

Banker profiles show role, experience and what the person can help with. A banker's name and contact details are shared only once they confirm your request. Bankers see the name of the student who requests a chat.

What does "former" mean on a profile?

Former bankers share first-hand experience from past roles, for example three years as an analyst in New York. They are labelled clearly, so you always know whether you are talking to someone who works there today.
